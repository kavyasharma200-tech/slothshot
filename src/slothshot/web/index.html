    When you go to bed, it wakes up.

    First it reads the screenshot with an open OCR model,

    then asks a 1B model what to call it.

    Gemma 3 1B, running in Ollama, on a laptop with no graphics card.

    It's slow, and often wrong.

    A name has to use words that are really on the screenshot, so every vague answer fails and Temporal asks again. Five tries in, it gives up and keeps the first words on the screen.

    Sometimes the second try is the one.

    Halfway through, I pulled the plug.

    A new sloth climbed back to the same notch.

    Temporal had kept every finished step, so the eight screenshots before it were never read again, and the call that died was retried 18 seconds later, once its heartbeat stopped.

    Ten and a half minutes, nineteen names.

    The drawing with no words on it was left where it was, since there was nothing to read.

    In the morning, say yes, or say nothing.

    The plan waits for you. If you're still asleep when the timer runs out, it takes your silence as a yes, and for a whole day after, one click puts every file back.

    This page is running on your own Slothshot, so you can do it for real.

    It never forgets, because Temporal writes everything down.

    The workflow only makes decisions, while the slow and flaky work happens in activities, and the moment one of them finishes, its result goes into the workflow's history, so a new worker can replay the night instead of living through it again.
